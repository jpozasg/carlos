Your writes go to a private **staging** area and are compared with the other run's later. You never see the other run's rows. Be thorough and cite sources: agreement between two runs is the evidence.

Staging rules:

Use it as `source_id`, as a level id (`gearbox_id: -12`), as `fixed_fault_id`, and in `PATCH /api/agent/<table>/-12` to correct it.
- Reuse what exists. If the real engine, generation or gearbox is already in the catalogue (`GET /api/engines`, `GET /api/generations`), PATCH the variant to point at it; do not create a second one. A level outside {SCOPE} is already researched: 403, leave it.

- A 409 on a fault, job or row is never ignored. Read the answer and do exactly one of: (1) the existing row is the same thing: PATCH it (`PATCH -12`, or the real id the server names) and extend it; (2) the existing row is a different thing: resend with `distinct_of=[<id>]` and `notes` giving the reason; (3) it is yours and wrong: PATCH it. Never resend an identical body.

What to research, per level in scope (protocol steps in brackets):
1. **Identity** (step 1). Variant: HSN, TSN, transmission, body style, emissions class, CO2, `gearbox_id`. TSN not confirmable from the ad: say *assumed* in `notes` and list the alternatives (one TSN per kW). Generation: start and end year of the whole production run, facelift year (or check `N/A`). Engine: the manufacturer's code (PATCH a placeholder 'UNKNOWN-…' or a descriptive name), fuel, displacement, power, aspiration, injection, timing drive; electric: usable battery kWh, AC/DC kW. Gearbox: code, type, gears. Where one applies: engine family, gearbox family, body style.
2. **Maintenance** (step 4). Every job of `config.JOBS` that applies, on its level, with `item(..., level=..., job='<JOB>')`: generation (`gen.hu` via `hu(v, level='generation')`, brake fluid, brakes, battery, wipers, cabin filter), engine (oil, air filter, spark plugs or fuel filter, timing belt or chain/coolant as a job or a check, electric: drive coolant, reduction gear oil, HV battery), gearbox (`gbx.service` GEARBOX_OIL, `gbx.clutch` CLUTCH on manual/DCT). Manufacturer normal-use interval (km and months); a shorter preventive interval only with a documented reason starting "Preventive interval:". A job any owner may do (`config.DIYABLE_JOBS`) gets both the DIY parts and the workshop price; workshop-only jobs the workshop price. **A price no source gives is estimated, never left NULL**: parts price plus labour hours x about 90 EUR/h, or a close generation of the same make, always `ESTIMATED` with the derivation in `notes`; never copy the DIY price into the workshop price. `VERIFIED` only with an A/B source. An electric car has no oil, filters, plugs or timing drive. Air, cabin and fuel filters, the 12 V battery and wiper blades are not contract items (#295, tiny cost effect): do not search for them separately, but when the schedule or price list you are already reading shows them, record them with their `job` code, interval and prices; they cost like any other job.
3. **Faults** (steps 2 and 5), for each part ENGINE, GEARBOX, CHASSIS, ELECTRICS. Per part consult **four sources** and record a `check(level, id, 'rel.<PART>.<source>', source_id, 'FOUND' | 'NONE FOUND', note)` for each: `adac` (ADAC Pannenstatistik), `tuev` (TÜV-Report), `guide` (a buying guide), and the specific one (`rel.ENGINE.engine_specific` on the engine family, `rel.GEARBOX.gearbox_specific` on the gearbox; CHASSIS and ELECTRICS use `guide`/`adac`/`tuev` plus the generation's `gen.risks`). A source that cannot be reached or has nothing is a `NONE FOUND` or `N/A` check with the reason, never a silent skip. Then file each failure with `fault(...)`: **`code=` is required** (a `component_code` from `fault_components` in the schema for its part; the code of the component that fails, not of the symptom; `OTHER` only with `notes` saying what it is), frequency band and shape, km window, a price (`ESTIMATED` with the derivation when nothing better), an `inspection_action` for everything checkable at a viewing, on the level the evidence names, no higher. Before filing look at `variant(<id>)` and at your staged rows: a similar fault is extended with a PATCH. One row per distinct failure; generic wear is a priced risk, not a weak point. Contract items: `eng.risks`, `fam.risks`, `gbx.risks`, `gen.risks`, `bdy.risks`, each with faults or a check `NONE FOUND`.
4. **Recalls**: KBA and manufacturer recall search, each recall one row in `recalls` on the level it names, with its build window (`applies_from`/`applies_to`) and an `inspection_action` "dealer VIN check"; then the check `gen.recalls`. `reliability_items` are refused. Generation, TÜV/ADAC baseline `gen.baseline` (`reliability_stats` per age class; record BOTH sources as published, the TÜV Report rows with the report's average as `class_average` and the ADAC rows without one (leave NULL, never invent an average); when only one source has a figure, also write the N/A check naming the other; if neither source has a figure for THIS generation record `check('generation', <id>, 'gen.baseline', source_id, 'N/A')` with what you tried, never another model's figures as a proxy; on disagreement write the less favourable figure, quote the other in `notes`, raise `SOURCES_DISAGREE`), `gen.safety` (Euro NCAP `ncap_stars`, `ncap_year`, or `N/A`).
5. **Variant data**: `var.consumption` with `consumption(v, value, n, lo, hi, source_id)`: the autokostencheck average of the exact HSN/TSN variant (sample size, min, max); electric: ev-database.org real consumption (Wh/km / 10) with `electric=True`; never Spritmonitor; if the page shows only the stored "Praxisverbrauch", write no value and record the check with the URL. `var.tyres` with `tyres(v, size, price_per_tyre, source_id, notes)` (one set of all-season tyres, median mid-range price). `var.gearbox` (an automatic gearbox's weak points for this variant, or a check).
6. **Disagreeing sources**: when two sources you read disagree, take the less favourable figure and raise a `SOURCES_DISAGREE` flag (`flag_review` rules in the protocol).
