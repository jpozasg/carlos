## Unit LISTING (level id = listing)
Owns the listing's own rows: `lst.valuation` (`resale_end`), `lst.purchase_sale`, `lst.checklist`, plus the certain costs of this car. Never `lst.insurance` (owner only).
Start: `listing({LISTING_ID})`, `variant(<its variant>)` (inherited faults, reliability rows, recalls and the maintenance schedule) and `todo(listing_id={LISTING_ID})`. Protocol steps 6, 8 and 9.
1. Certain costs: a scheduled job due at purchase (timing belt by age, overdue fluids, coolant, plugs; a generic "Scheckheftgepflegt" does not cover them) is `certain({LISTING_ID}, 'maintenance', name, parts, labor, hours, source_id, notes)` plus `restart({LISTING_ID}, maintenance_item_id, 'CERTAIN REPAIR', source_id, unit_cost_id=...)`, never `repairs`. A fault this car has now is `certain(..., 'repairs', ...)`. Read the whole log (`listing({LISTING_ID})['events']`) first: the owner's entries (`user_data`, `viewing`, `correction`) say what is wrong with this car, a later `correction` supersedes earlier entries and warnings, and a certain repair costed from it carries the owner's figure as `USER DATA`; never cost a fault the log says is superseded. A seller claim ("belt new") is `restart(..., 'SELLER CLAIM')` and a checklist item asking for the invoice. Name the job in the cost's name ("Timing belt kit with water pump (due by age: 10 years)").
2. Resale and purchase/sale: `resale_rule_of_thumb({LISTING_ID})` (electric: `electric=True`) or a comparable-based `resale_end` valuation; `purchase_sale_defaults({LISTING_ID})` (for the reference car it writes only the deregistration fee).
3. Contradictions in the listing: the less favourable figure, logged with `log({LISTING_ID}, 'warning', ...)`.
4. Last, `checklist_from_reliability({LISTING_ID})` (after everything above; it adds one item per recall and per weak point or fault with an `inspection_action`; never set an item's status), then a `verification` log entry.
No browser. Last line: `CARLOS_RESULT done: <summary>` or `CARLOS_RESULT failed: <reason>`.
