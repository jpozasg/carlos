# Ranking: formula and objectivity rules

The ranking answers one question: *given what matters to this account, which car for sale looks best?* Redesigned in #169 (docs/target-design.md §6): every criterion has a **fixed score curve** 0–100, so a car's points depend only on the car (the earlier min–max among the candidates reshuffled 76 of 300 pairs when one car was added). The reference car is scored exactly the same way and listed at its score, never ranked.

**A car is scored only when it is complete:** every criterion with a weight > 0 has a value **and** no required research item of the car (its levels and the listing, docs/cost-research-protocol.md) is MISSING. Otherwise `score` is null and `not_scored` says what is missing (`criteria`, `research`). Cars over the **maximum budget** are not ranked (`excluded: 'over_max'`) unless the account switches it off (`exclude_over_max = 0`; then they get 0 budget points). Confidence (the share of research done) is shown, never scored.

## Criteria and their curves
| Key | Value | Points |
|---|---|---|
| `cost_month` | complete TCO ÷ months: fuel, insurance, tax, maintenance, repairs, tyres, purchase/sale, depreciation | in percent against an **anchor** = the current car's cost per month, else the candidates' median: `60 − 112·ln(cost ÷ anchor)`, 0–100 (≈ +10.7 points per 10 % cheaper). The anchor can shift every shown score by the same amount when cars are added, but never changes the order |
| `budget` (replaces `cash_now`) | asking price | 100 up to 55 % of the ideal budget, linear to 80 at the ideal, then `80·(1 − ((price − ideal) ÷ (max − ideal))²)` (falling faster and faster) to 0 at the maximum (1.5 × the ideal without a maximum); above the maximum excluded (switchable) |
| `mileage` | km | ≤ 50,000 = 100; 150,000 = 50; ≥ 250,000 = 0 (linear between) |
| `age` | years since first registration | ≤ 3 = 100; 10 = 50; ≥ 20 = 0 |
| `reliability` | one value: researched weak points (each part starts at 9 and loses the severity points of its 3 worst weak points; out of 36) and the TÜV/ADAC figure of the model at the car's age class ÷ the class average | `2/3 × weak points ÷ 36 × 100 + 1/3 × curve(ratio: 0.5 = 100, 1 = 50, 2 = 0)`; unknown without either |
| `inspection` | checklist items marked PROBLEM (recall checks excluded) | 100 − 25 per problem |
| `power` | PS | in percent against the current car (else the median): `60 + 112·ln(PS ÷ anchor)`, 0–100 |
| `safety` | Euro NCAP stars of the generation (`ncap_stars`); a rating before 2009 (`ncap_year`) counts one star less, no ESP (`vehicles.esp = 0`) one star less | 5 = 100, 4 = 70, 3 = 40, 2 = 25, 1 = 10, 0 = 0 |
| `distance` | km from the account's postcode to the car (`listings.distance_km`, per account) | ≤ 25 = 100; 100 = 50; ≥ 200 = 0 |

**Filters** (Settings, preferences `filter_*`): seller type, warranty required, service history (documented, or documented or claimed), accident-free (confirmed, or confirmed or claimed), HU at least N months left. A car that fails one, or whose ad does not say, is not ranked (`excluded: 'filter:<name>'`). **Distance** = straight-line km between the account's postcode and the listing's `postal_code`, from the bundled table `carlos/plz_de.csv` (one point per postcode; data © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0), computed live so a new postcode applies at once; `listings.distance_km`, when set, overrides it; the reference car is at 0 km.

Car score = `Σ weight × points ÷ Σ weights` over the weighted criteria (all present by the completeness rule). `GET /api/tco` returns per car `score`, `rank`, `excluded`, `not_scored`, `score_parts` and `explain` (value, weight, points, contribution, the anchor for cost and power, why a criterion is missing). Price, consumption, insurance and tax stay information only (inside `cost_month`); `cash_now` and `confidence` remain metrics, not criteria.

## Weights
Weights are integers 0–5 per criterion. They come from the questionnaire (`ranking_answers`), or are set by hand (`ranking_weights`); the API then reports `manual: true` and keeps the derived weights for comparison; `ranking_weights: null` returns to the answers.

### The questionnaire: three to six best–worst choices (#169)
Since issue #19 the weights come from **best–worst scaling** (MaxDiff), not from scenario questions. Since issues #54 and #64 (eight criteria) each of eight tasks shows four cars of the same model that are equal in everything except one advantage each, as a small table (four rows, four cars, the advantage highlighted). The person taps the car they would buy first, then the one they would buy last; "can't decide" skips a task without counting it.

**Why this method** (four independent reviews, 2026-11-05: survey methodology, form usability, German used-car market, a simulation red-team):
- Direct importance ratings come out flat ("everything matters") and suffer from acquiescence and scale-use bias; forcing a choice discriminates better ([Lee, Soutar & Louviere, *Best–worst scaling*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/bestworst-scaling/bestworst-scaling-an-alternative-to-ratings-data/756EDFCDC2DFF6C58A8198F8DB95C526); [Orme, Sawtooth](https://content.sawtoothsoftware.com/assets/f39e8184-8c93-4b3b-a9a3-735cc107907a)).
- Disguised (projective) questions reduce social-desirability bias only on socially sensitive topics ([Fisher 1993](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/20/2/303/1793106)); car priorities are not, so the earlier "life scenario" items added an interpretive leap (e.g. "invest a windfall" → monthly cost) without a benefit. Choices between concrete cars stay indirect (nobody is asked "how important is X") but every answer has an obvious meaning.
- Paired, realistic choices predict real behaviour well and people tolerate many simple repeated tasks ([Hainmueller, Hangartner & Yamamoto 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4345583); [Bansak et al. 2018](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/118642)).
- Burden: 3–5 items per set, each item shown 2–3 times ([Sawtooth MaxDiff design](https://sawtoothsoftware.com/help/lighthouse-studio/manual/maxdiff-designing-study.html)); pairwise AHP would need 36 judgments for nine criteria and constant-sum allocation breaks down beyond about six items.
- Weights depend on the ranges shown ([Fischer 1995](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/771230)), so tasks show concrete magnitudes.

**Design (#169, shorter).** The nine criteria (cost per month, budget, mileage, age, reliability, inspection, power, safety, distance) sit on a 3 × 3 grid (AG(2,3)); each task shows the **six** cars outside one row or one column, so six tasks show every criterion four times and every pair at least twice. The three row tasks come first: when their answers are consistent (no criterion picked both first and last) the questionnaire stops after three screens, otherwise it continues to six; the Ranking page then shows the weights to confirm or adjust (sliders). The cost advantage is shown in percent ("12 % less per month") to match the percent scoring of cost. Task ids `u1`–`u6`; earlier answers (`r…`, `s…`) retire and the questionnaire is asked again.

**Simulation check (#169, in the tests).** 300 simulated respondents with random true priorities answering every task by their priorities: the favourite always gets weight 5 and the least important 1; fewer than 1 % of criterion pairs come out inverted (an offline run of 2,000 gave 0.2 %, always by one step).

**Scoring.** Per criterion: `s = (times picked first − times picked last) ÷ times answered` (−1…+1), weight = `max(1, int(5 × (s + 1) / 2 + 0.5))` (first every time = 5, always in the middle = 3, last every time = 1: answers never switch a criterion off; only a hand-set 0 does). A criterion never answered gets 3 (neutral); no answers at all = all 3. This is an absolute scale, not "the strongest scaled to 5", so mild preferences stay mild. `GET /api/ranking` returns the `tally` (shown, first, last) per criterion, and the UI explains every weight with it ("shown 3×: bought first 2×, last 0×"). Stored answers: `{"r1": "power>mileage", …}` (first > last); the `t…` (nine criteria) and `q…` (fourteen tasks) answers are retired. Answers to retired questionnaires are ignored and reported as `retired_answers: true`, and the UI asks for the new choices.

## Objectivity rules for agents (the same for every car)
The score is only as fair as the data. These rules keep the inputs comparable:
1. **Same sources, same depth.** Reliability research follows `cost-research-protocol.md` step 2 for every car: ADAC Pannenstatistik/Gebrauchtwagen-Info, TÜV-Report, one buying guide, one engine-specific source. Do not add ten forum anecdotes for one car and nothing for the next. Since issue #54 only the 3 worst points per part count, so depth no longer inflates the score, but a part nobody researched keeps its full 9 (the to-do lists it): research all four parts.
2. **Severity by rule, not by mood.** `HIGH`: can cause engine/gearbox failure or a cost above about 1,500 €; `MEDIUM`: a repair of 300–1,500 € or a safety-relevant defect; `LOW-MEDIUM`: 100–300 €; `LOW`: cosmetic or under 100 €; `HIGH IF APPLICABLE`: a recall or batch problem to check by VIN (`is_recall=1` for a manufacturer recall). One row per distinct failure, recalls included (each recall is its own row: they show how the model came out); no duplicates across levels. Every row has a `part`.
3. **Statuses by rule.** `VERIFIED` only with an A/B source for this engine or variant; `ESTIMATED` whenever a figure was derived or taken from a C/D source, with the derivation in `notes`; `PENDING` never with a number.
4. **Source quality by rule.** A: official (manufacturer schedule, BMF calculator, KBA, fee tables). B: ADAC, TÜV, Haynes, dealer tool, comparison quote. C: aggregators, encyclopedias, consumption databases. D: forums, search snippets, single retailers.
5. **Engine data** (aspiration, injection, timing drive) comes from the manufacturer or ADAC description and identifies the engine; it is not scored (issue #54), and unknown stays NULL.
6. **Every risk priced** (estimate when needed) so `cost_month` exists for every car; a car without a complete TCO has no `cost_month` and a lower coverage, which is honest rather than punitive.
7. **Listing contradictions**: the less favourable figure (mileage, CO2), logged as a `warning`.
